Nangur Villa: interior brief and 3D walkthrough

A working draft for the family, the architect, the carpenter and the interior designers. The house is a traditional Sri Vaishnava family home in Nangur village near Sirkazhi, built for elders first: every ground floor space reachable by wheelchair, a lift to the upper floors, and a 100-seat terrace for festival days. The brief asks for interiors that are elegant and calm, never flashy, and sensible on cost.

The place, and what it asks of the house

Nangur lies about 11 km south-east of Sirkazhi in the Cauvery delta, among paddy fields, with eleven Vishnu temples (Divya Desams) in and around the village. These are the conditions the interiors have to live with.

What the place is likeWhat it means for the interiors
Hot and humid for most of the year; long summers, short wintersHigh ceilings, cross ventilation, ceiling fans in every room in addition to AC, lime plaster that breathes, cotton and cane rather than heavy upholstery

About 13 km from the seaSalt in the air: powder-coated aluminum, brass and SS 304 fittings outside; AC outdoor units with coated coils, kept under shade on a service ledge

The Sri Vaishnava thread through the house

The eleven Nangur temples were sung by Thirumangai Azhwar, whose horse was Adalma, and the family's own guide is Sri Ramanujar (Udayavar). Srirangam is the model: one idea in each place, the richness kept for the door, the lift and the pooja, and plain lime walls everywhere else.

Traditional touches, in short

  • Red oxide floors with Athangudi insets, lime-plastered walls, exposed teak rafters in the double-height living.
  • Thinnai seating at the verandah, oonjal in the pooja hall and the sitouts, tulasi madam in the rear courtyard, kolam at the door.
  • Tanjore paintings, brass lamps and vessels, stone and bronze sculpture, cane and teak furniture, cotton and silk fabrics.
  • Jaali screens beside the main door and between the pooja hall and the dining, so light and air pass and lamps glow through.
